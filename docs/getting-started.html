Start here / ARGOS 0.4.1

Your first observing session.

Setup, connection, field identification, sequence and review, for ARGOS 0.4.1 on a Seestar.

Scope

ARGOS acquires raw FITS and plots a live differential preview. It does not calibrate, register or produce a final light curve — that stays in Siril and star_var_script.

There is no unattended mode in 0.4.1: no weather safety, no restart recovery. Stay with the telescope.

First

Prepare the telescope in its official app

For now, start every observing night in the official Seestar app. Power and initialise the telescope there, then complete its GoTo calibration before connecting ARGOS.

Current operating procedureARGOS does not replace the official app’s startup and pointing calibration yet. Do the initial focus in the official app as well. ARGOS can focus and refocus later in the session, but its current autofocus is slower; use it when needed rather than as the first focus of the night.

01

Before going outside

Do this indoors, with internet: catalogue lookups are cached and reused in the field.

02

Connect and acquire a first frame

  1. Power the Seestar and join its network, or put the telescope and computer on the same local network.
  2. Open Connection in the sidebar. Enter the Alpaca address and port — 10.0.0.1 and 32323 in Seestar access-point mode — or use Discover.
  3. Select the telescope profile, then choose Connect equipment. Telescope, Camera, Filter Wheel and Focuser must all read Ready. If one does not, open Show connection and device details to reach each device separately.
  4. In Observe, acquire a short light frame and confirm the saved FITS shows the field you expect.
Display ≠ dataDebayer mode, stretch and channel are preview-only. The FITS on disk stays linear 16-bit CFA, BAYERPAT='GRBG'.

03

Identify the field and choose the stars

  1. With a full-resolution frame open, choose Field → Identify field. ARGOS runs the ASTAP configured in Settings → Astrometry, solving on the extracted green plane.
  2. Compare the solved centre, scale and grid against recognisable stars. If markers consistently miss the centroids, stop — the role buttons stay disabled until the field is solved, and a wrong WCS gives a wrong identity.
  3. Set the layer chips and the Gaia G limit slider. Leave Labels off in a crowded field.
  4. Click the target, then press Target on its information card.
  5. Add comparison stars — at least two, or measurements are flagged only N comparison(s). Then add a Check star: a star expected to be constant, measured against the ensemble to expose its drift.

For the layer chips, catalogue depth and what an unmatched source means, read Identify a solved field.

04

Plan and run the sequence

  1. In Plan, search the designation in Target search. Messier, NGC and IC resolve from the bundled catalogue; other names go through CDS Sesame once, then cache. The result does not slew — command the GoTo from the Observe telescope panel, or point with Stellarium.
  2. For a transit, type the planet into Find planet to pull the published NASA Exoplanet Archive ephemeris, then set the pre- and post-transit Baseline.
  3. Check Target visibility: the dashed line is 30° altitude over the coming local night, computed for the site in Settings. The estimated time excludes downloads and autofocus, so leave margin.
  4. Build the sequence: frame type, filter, exposure, gain, count and interval. Keep the acquisition settings stable during a time-series run.
  5. In Acquisition options, set Target name (FITS). That string — not the session name — is written to OBJECT, into the filenames and into the target set. Repeat, autofocus cadence and end action are here too; Presets saves the plan as JSON.
  6. Start the sequence. Pause finishes the current frame then holds; Stop ends the run. On the live curve, a shift common to target and comparisons means cloud, tracking, focus or saturation — not variability.

05

Review and hand off the data

  1. Open Review in the sidebar and press Open session…. Choose the folder holding session.json — <timestamp>_<OBJECT>/, not the lights/ subfolder.
  2. Check frame count, filters and warnings, then the FWHM, HFD, sky-background and temperature trends.
  3. Hover a curve point for its values; select it and press Open source frame to open that FITS. Nothing is written back.
  4. Export measurements… writes target, check and comparisons to CSV, which Review can reopen. Export target (AAVSO)… writes the science target only — comparison curves are diagnostics and must never be submitted as observations.
  5. Copy the whole session folder before reducing it in Siril and star_var_script.

06

When something does not work

First checks before reporting an issue
SymptomFirst action
ASTAP does not solveConfirm Settings → Astrometry reports both the executable and the database folder. Retry on a full-resolution frame and note the exact ASTAP message.
Role buttons greyed outThe field is not solved, or the clicked source has no reliable coordinates. Run Field → Identify field first.
Marker on empty skyDo not assign a role. Recheck the ASTAP centre, scale and rotation on a full-resolution frame.
Too few comparisonsRaise the Gaia G limit, check the field-identification budget in Catalogues & depth…, then pick candidates manually.
