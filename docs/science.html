Science

What the number means

Any program can print a magnitude. The question that matters is what stands behind it — which pixels were summed, which noise sources were counted, which stars set the zero point, and how much of the scatter the error bar does not explain. This page is the answer, stated precisely enough to be argued with.

The measurement chain

From photons on the sensor to one point on a light curve. Every stage runs on your laptop, frame by frame, while the subs are still landing.

Raw framelinear · 16-bit · GRBG
Nothing is stretched, debayered or rescaled on the way to disk. The saved FITS is the sensor. What you see on screen comes from a display pipeline that never touches the data pipeline — the single rule the rest of this page depends on.
Green plane(G1+G2)/2 · half res
Every measurement happens on one grid: the green half-resolution plane, one sample per 2×2 Bayer tile, averaging both green photosites for signal-to-noise. Star detection, plate solving, the WCS and catalogue projection all speak that same grid, so a marker cannot drift from the pixel it describes.
Aperturecircle + sky annulus
A circular aperture, a median sky annulus, a background-subtracted sum. Saturation is tested against the profile’s linearity threshold rather than full well, and a peak with no PSF support around it is flagged suspect instead of being quietly measured — that is a hot pixel or a cosmic ray, not a star.
Photon budgetCCD equation, in e−
Source shot noise, sky, and read noise, converted to electrons through the sensor’s e−/ADU. This is the formal error: the part of the scatter that is fully explained by counting statistics.
Zero pointcomparison ensemble
The mean of (catalogue − instrumental) over the comparison stars, with a median-and-MAD clip that removes a variable or blended comparison before it contaminates the target.
Systematic floorrun level
Scintillation, flat-field residuals, thin cirrus and tracking drift are shared across a run and appear nowhere in the photon budget. Argos measures them from the light curve itself and adds them in quadrature, so the plotted error bar reflects the night rather than the arithmetic.
Time & airmassDATE-AVG · Pickering 2002
Timestamps are exposure midpoints, not shutter-open. Airmass comes from one formula used project-wide, so a frame’s FITS header and its light-curve point can never disagree. BJD_TDB is a post-processing step, and Argos says so rather than approximating it.

Four equations

The whole photometric contract, written out. These are the expressions the code evaluates — not a simplified account of them.

Instrumental magnitude and its formal error
m_inst = −2.5 · log₁₀(F)
σ_m    = 1.0857 · σ_F / F

F is the background-subtracted aperture sum. The magnitude is uncalibrated and carries no zero point — it is only ever used differentially.

The CCD equation — every noise source, in electrons
σ_F = √( F + n_pix · ( S + R² ) )

F source electrons, n_pix aperture pixels, S sky electrons per pixel, R read noise in electrons. Signal-to-noise is the ratio of the two. Nothing here is a fitted fudge factor; every term is a counted quantity.

Ensemble zero point, with outlier rejection
ZP = mean( m_cat − m_inst )  over kept comparisons
reject  |Δ − median| > max( 2.5 · 1.4826 · MAD, 10 mmag )

Median and MAD rather than mean and RMS, deliberately: in a small ensemble a single bad comparison inflates the RMS enough to hide itself. The 10 mmag floor stops photon noise alone from rejecting a perfectly good star when three or four comparisons are nearly identical.

The systematic floor — what the photons do not explain
Var( mᵢ₋₁ − 2mᵢ + mᵢ₊₁ ) = 6σ²
σ_sys = √( σ_real² − median(σ_formal)² )
σ_final = hypot( σ_formal, σ_sys )

Second differences cancel any locally linear trend, so a genuinely varying star does not inflate its own noise estimate. What remains after the formal error is subtracted in quadrature is the systematic floor. The same contract is used by star_var_script, so a live Argos error bar and a reduced one mean the same thing.

And one path with no catalogue at all

For a transit, absolute calibration is beside the point. The relative-flux mode plots F_target / ∑ F_comparisons, which needs no catalogue magnitudes and no zero point — only a stable ensemble and a stable cadence. It is normalised to the displayed series median, and it is deliberately not an out-of-transit normalisation or a detrending result.

How we check it is true

A pipeline that only agrees with itself has proved nothing. The test is an external one: measure a star whose behaviour was published by someone else, and see whether the points fall on their curve.

RR Lyrae is the reference target, and it is a demanding one. It swings roughly a magnitude in under half a day, with a steep rise and a slow decline — a shape that punishes timing errors, zero-point drift and airmass mistakes in visibly different ways. Its period and template light curve are published, so the comparison is against the literature rather than against a previous Argos run.

The protocol is deliberately reproducible by anyone with a Seestar: acquire an uninterrupted series, select the variable and its comparison stars from the AAVSO chart, reduce the raw subs in Siril with star_var_script, phase-fold at the published period, and overlay the Sesar 2010 r-band template. No free parameters are fitted to make it match: the period and the template come from the paper, and the observed segment either lands on the model or it does not.

What counts as a pass
  • The phase-folded points follow the template through the rising branch — the part of the curve where an error in time or zero point shows up first.
  • The measured per-point scatter is consistent with the quoted error bars, once the systematic floor is included.
  • The same night, reduced independently in Siril, agrees with the live Argos quick-look within that scatter.

What Argos does not claim

The limits are part of the specification. A tool that overstates what it measured is worse than one that measures less.

Read this before quoting a number
  • No calibration frames are applied. Argos does not subtract darks or divide by flats. Its live curve is a raw-sub quick-look, and flat-field residuals are part of what the systematic floor is absorbing.
  • The live curve is not a publishable curve. It exists so you can see in the field that the target, the ensemble and the cadence are behaving. The publishable result comes from reducing the raw subs in Siril.
  • No transit fitting. Depth, duration and mid-point are never derived from Argos data; the ephemeris shown is the published one, used for planning.
  • BJD_TDB is not computed. Timestamps are UTC exposure midpoints. The barycentric correction belongs to post-processing, and Argos refuses an ephemeris whose time standard is not explicitly BJD_TDB rather than converting implicitly.
  • Only the S30 Pro is validated. The S30 and S50 profiles carry published optics but unconfirmed CFA layout and gain scaling, and must not be used for precision photometry yet — see Hardware.
  • 0.4.1 is a field-validation alpha. Keep your raw FITS. Review the results independently. This is not an unattended-observatory release.

None of this is an apology. Argos stops precisely where honesty stops, and hands over real subs with complete headers — which is what makes the next stage possible instead of guesswork.

References

The published work the pipeline leans on, and the catalogues it queries.

  • Honeycutt 1992 CCD ensemble photometry on an inhomogeneous set of exposures. The zero point from a comparison ensemble, rather than a single comparison star.
  • Sesar et al. 2010 Light-curve templates for RR Lyrae stars. The external reference the validation curve is laid over.
  • Pickering 2002 Airmass interpolation. One formula for the FITS header and the light-curve point alike.
  • AAVSO VSX / VSP Variable Star Index and Variable Star Plotter. Target identification and the comparison-star chart, queried on request and cached locally.
  • NASA Exoplanet Archive PSCompPars. Published ephemerides for transit planning — queried only when you ask for a planet, then cached.
  • ASTAP Plate solving. External and never bundled; it supplies the WCS that puts every measured star on the sky.
  • star_var_script Siril reduction script. The post-processing half of the workflow, and the source of the shared uncertainty contract. On GitHub →
