ARGOS 0.5.0  /  first beta  /  free software, GNU GPL v3

Put your smart telescope to scientific work.

A desktop app for variable-star observing with ZWO Seestar telescopes. Plan a timed run, watch the light curve grow as the frames arrive, check every point against the frame that made it, and export the AAVSO report.

Its own software stops at the picture. ARGOS starts at the measurement.

Download 0.5.0 → Documentation

macOS on Apple silicon · Linux x86_64 (.deb, technical preview) · installers and release notes

First beta. A new desktop interface, and photometry checked against an independent Siril reduction, astropy and the AAVSO catalogues. Tested with the built-in simulator and with replays of real nights, not yet live at the telescope: watch your first night closely. Patches 0.5.x will follow.

01 · Your data

Raw frames, kept as they came.

Every frame is written once, as the sensor read it, with a header that says when, where and how it was taken. Nothing in ARGOS ever modifies it: re-run the photometry next year with better code, and the photons are still there.

DATE-AVG

The middle of the exposure, the time a light curve needs. Curves also carry JD and BJDTDB for timings; your computer’s clock sets their accuracy.

BAYERPAT

The colour mosaic stays as recorded, its pattern declared. ARGOS measures the green pixels, the closest to the V band; any tool can do the same.

EGAIN RDNOISE

The camera's gain and read noise, measured on an S30 Pro (0.228 e−/ADU and 2.6 e− at gain 80): the error bars come from the sensor's real noise.

FOCALLEN XPIXSZ

Focal length and pixel size, so any plate solver or photometry tool knows the scale: about 3.7″ per pixel on an S30 Pro.

AIRMASS OBJECT

The airmass of every frame from the mount, with the target, the site and the observer: what a report asks for, without a logbook.

IMAGETYP

Light, dark, flat or bias, in the folders Siril expects: point Siril at the night's folder and go.

Raw16-bit FITS as the sensor read it: no debayer, no stretch, no stack
Yourson your disk: no account, no cloud, no telemetry
Openstandard FITS and CSV: Siril, Python, a spreadsheet

Every keyword is listed in the FITS reference. The stretch, zoom and colours on screen change only what you see, never the file.

02 · The night

One night, one session folder.

Five screens in one window take you from the plan to the report, and everything runs on your own computer.

Before the night

Plan it indoors.

Type the star's name: ARGOS resolves it and draws its altitude through the night at your site, with the planned run on it. Set the steps (lights, darks, flats, biases), each with its exposure, gain, count and cadence; the run's length shows before you start.

For an exoplanet, type the planet instead: ARGOS predicts the night's transit from the NASA Exoplanet Archive and builds a series that covers it, with a baseline on each side.

Before the start, a check lists what is missing, such as tonight's darks, flats and biases, and whether the target stays above your altitude limit.

During the night

Watch the run, keep your night vision.

Observe in Run mode, at the top of this page, follows the run frame by frame: the live curve of the target and of the check star, and the run's progress. Watch mode shows the essentials in large type from across the room, and ARGOS raises an alert when frames stop arriving, the stars swell or thin out, or the target sinks toward your altitude limit.

Every screen comes in three dark themes. Night Red, shown here on Review, keeps your eyes dark-adapted: nothing on the screen emits anything but red light, checked pixel by pixel on every capture, this one included.

After the night

Read it back, star by star.

Review lists every session folder and opens one as a wall of plots, as above: the target, the check star, each comparison on its own, and the frames' HFD. Click a point to read every star at that time, and open the frame behind it. Doubtful points are marked, never hidden: saturated, suspect, no detection.

Re-run the photometry on the raw frames with today's code: it writes a new folder in the session and never rewrites a file.

The report

Export it for the AAVSO.

One filter per report, target rows only, with the check star (KNAME, KMAG), the comparisons' VSP chart and the version in #SOFTWARE. Saturated, suspect and no-detection points stay out of the file, and the dialog counts them.

The rows are untransformed green (FILT TG, TRANS NO) measured on raw frames: calibration is planned for 0.6. The dialog says so, and the guide tells you when a report is worth sending and what to write in its notes (can I submit this?).

03 · Field identification

Know what is in the frame.

Solve it with ASTAP, then let the catalogues name the stars and propose the comparisons.

01

Plate solving

ASTAP finds where the telescope points. During a run the apertures follow the field on anchor stars as it drifts and turns (as it does on an alt-azimuth mount), and a frame where the stars do not line up gets no point rather than a wrong one.

02

Catalogues on the image

AAVSO VSX for the variables and VSP for the comparison sequences, Gaia DR3 for the field stars and their proper motions, SIMBAD for names, the NASA Exoplanet Archive for transit hosts. Cached on disk.

03

The ensemble, proposed

ARGOS takes the VSP sequence, measures each candidate on the frame and proposes the comparison stars; a check star stays out of the ensemble. Every choice can be changed.

04 · One night, checked

The same night, reduced twice.

ARGOS keeps every raw frame, so the same night can also go through Siril, which registers and measures the frames (and calibrates them when given darks, flats and biases), driven by its star_var_script extension. Frame by frame the two agree within 0.008 mag: here is RR Lyr on 2026-09-25, re-run by ARGOS 0.5.0 and reduced by star_var_script with the same two comparison stars.

01 · InputThe same raw frames

195 frames of 10 s and the same two comparison stars; neither side used darks or flats.

02 · CodeA separate reduction

Siril registers and measures the frames, driven by star_var_script; ARGOS uses neither. Same inputs, separate measurement code.

03 · ScopeThe measurement, not the calibration

The shared noise cancels in the difference: it checks ARGOS's photometry, not the sky or the flat field. Calibration is planned for 0.6.

One night, one star, one instrument. The error bars are on the safe side for bright stars like these. Every figure, with its method and what was not checked: how the results were checked.
