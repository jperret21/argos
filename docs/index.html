ARGOS 0.4.1  /  free software, GNU GPL v3

Put your smart telescope to scientific work.

A desktop workspace for variable-star observing with ZWO Seestar telescopes. Record a timed sequence, follow relative brightness as the frames arrive, and review each point against the image that produced it.

A Seestar is a capable little astrograph. Its own software is built to produce a picture — it stops where a measurement would begin. ARGOS picks it up there.

01 · Why it exists

An image is not a measurement.

A photograph and a data point can be the very same pixels. What separates them is everything recorded around the pixels — and once a frame has been debayered, stretched or stacked, none of it can be recovered.

The clock

The exposure midpoint, to the second — DATE-AVG, MJD-AVG, and BJD_TDB once the site is known. A light curve on the wrong clock is a wrong light curve, and no amount of reprocessing fixes it afterwards.

The optics

FOCALLEN, APTDIA, FOCRATIO, XPIXSZ. Without them the plate scale is a guess, and an aperture sized in pixels is the wrong size on the sky.

The detector

EGAIN and RDNOISE — electrons per ADU, and read noise. There is no uncertainty without these: the CCD equation counts electrons, and a raw file counts ADU.

The sky

AIRMASS, MOONSEP, MOONALT, MOONPHAS. What the atmosphere and the Moon were doing while the shutter was open.

The mosaic, untouched

BAYERPAT='GRBG' over a linear 16-bit array. ARGOS never debayers, stretches or scales what it writes — the display pipeline works on a copy.

Sixty-five keywords per frame, every one of them listed and explained in the FITS reference. They are written because something downstream needs them, not for completeness.

02 · What it does

One night, one session folder.

Built for three kinds of evening: following a variable star well enough to report it, sitting on an exoplanet transit for a few hours, and knowing before you pack up whether the night was any good.

01

Identify the field

ASTAP solves the frame. Gaia DR3, AAVSO VSX, SIMBAD and VSP name what is in it.

02

Record the sequence

Set exposure and cadence. Every frame is written as linear 16-bit FITS with correct optics headers.

03

Review offline

Open any curve point beside its source image, with focus, background and star count.

04 · One result

A rising branch, on the published template.

What ARGOS writes is scientifically usable data: raw, linear sub-exposures carrying every header a measurement needs. Calibrated and registered in Siril, then reduced with star_var_script, they yield a light curve of scientific quality — this one, folded on its catalogue period, falls on a published template.

01 · ARGOSRecords

Timed FITS frames, a live differential curve, and the run-level uncertainty floor.

02 · SirilCalibrates and registers

Dark, flat and bias calibration, then registration of the untouched raw frames.

03 · star_var_scriptReduces

Final photometry and the AAVSO export. ARGOS applies the same systematic-floor model live, so both share one uncertainty contract.

The curve you watch during the night is a quick-look on uncalibrated subs: it tells you the run is working, not what the star did. The measurement comes out of the reduction — which is why ARGOS keeps every raw frame, and why that reduction stays independent and auditable.
